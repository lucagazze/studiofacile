The real problem

The Pre-Trip Is Not a Memory Test

You have been handed a list of a hundred parts and told to learn it. That is why it feels impossible — and why people who studied for three weeks still blank out in front of the examiner.

Here is the thing nobody tells you: almost every part is checked the same way. Eighteen wheels, one pattern. Every hose, one pattern. Every bracket, one pattern. And about every single part you say the same two things: what condition it is in, and whether it is held on properly.

Learn eight patterns and the hundred parts stop being a list. They become things you recognise.

You are not trying to remember.
You are trying to see.

How this book was made

Every Number Checked Against Five State Handbooks

Not a blog, not a practice app: the official manuals the test is written from. Each figure was looked up, one by one, before the page was drawn.

That is not a slogan — it changed the book. The low air pressure warning, for example, does not say the same thing everywhere: most manuals say the signal must come on before pressure falls below 55 psi, New York says below 60, and California words it as a band from 55 to 75. A book that prints one of those as the answer is wrong in most of the country.

So that page does not print a number. It shows you that it varies, and leaves you a space to write your own state's.

Your examiner's score sheet always wins.

Before You Decide

Is this the written test or the driving test?

Neither. This is the vehicle inspection — the pre-trip — which is the first of the three parts of the skills test, and the one most people fail. The two bonus books cover the other two parts.

I am in a different state. Does it still work?

Yes, and it is built for that. Every figure was checked against the official handbooks of five states. Where they all agree, the number is printed. Where they do not — the low air warning is the clearest case — the page shows you that it varies and leaves you a space to write your own state's number. Your examiner's score sheet always wins, and the book says so on page three.

Do I still need my school?

Yes. This is not a course and it does not give you a licence or a certificate. It is the illustrated material that goes with the training you are already paying for.

How do I get it?

Can I print it?

Yes, as many times as you want, for yourself. The last six pages are made to be printed.

Is it only for Class A?

It is written for the Class A combination vehicle, because that is the longest inspection and the one with the coupling and the trailer. If you are testing in a straight truck, the engine, cab, brake and walk-around parts are the same; the coupling and trailer part will not apply to you.
